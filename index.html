The Migration Reality

Assam's youth already power India's frontline. Today, unskilled and unseen.

Assam's people want to work — its labour-force participation is among India's highest. With too few local jobs, hundreds of thousands migrate for construction, plywood, hotels, security and factory floors across the country.

They leave as helpers, not skilled workers — and the state has no way to see, support or count them. Change that, and migration becomes an opportunity, not a loss.

The Support Gap

They migrate on their own — unskilled, unverified, unsupported.

The gap isn't effort — Assam's workers work hard. The gap is that no skills profile, no verification and no line of support travels with them. So they start at the bottom, stay informal, and neither they nor the state can build on the journey.

→No skills profile. They arrive able to work, but with nothing to prove it — so they enter as helpers.
→No verification. No way to show a better employer they're trained, reliable and job-ready.
→No support that travels. Guidance, welfare and documents stay behind in Assam.
Give a worker skills, proof and support that travel with them — and the same migration lifts them up instead of holding them down.

Support Through the Journey

Support that travels with the worker.

Skills alone aren't enough. A migrating worker needs benefits, money and someone to call that move with them — so the journey is supported end to end, not left to chance.

◆Portable welfare & insurance

Social security that crosses state borders — accident & health cover from day one, benefits that don't reset when a worker moves.

◆Financial inclusion

Bank-linked wages, savings, easy remittance home and early-wage access — so families are supported, not indebted (betterMoney).

◆A helpline in their language

Verified employers and an Assamese grievance line — so a worker always has someone to call, and a fake job can be flagged early.

◆Guidance, before & after placement

Pre-departure orientation, documents in order and regular check-ins — support that doesn't stop at the certificate.

The Readiness Gap

The plants are coming. Skill for them the right way.

India's experience is consistent — state skilling built without live employer demand produces certificates, not jobs. Karnataka's own audit found just 18% of trainees placed against a 70% target, courses chosen without vacancy evidence.

Assam can leapfrog that mistake: wire skilling to the named, dated demand of Jagiroad, the refineries and the logistics parks — before they switch on.

BetterPlace's model is the inversion: demand first, placement verified, paid on outcomes.

Role-level demand mapping

Cleanroom & assembly operators, industrial electricians, welders, warehouse associates, hospitality staff — the exact roles the pipeline hires, mapped district by district.

Women-first pipelines

Electronics assembly is a proven women's-employment engine across Asia. Assam can design women-first tracks into Jagiroad from day one.

Timing is everything

Skilling has a lead time. Plants hiring in 12–24 months need sourcing & training started now.

Sources & Notes

The evidence behind this proposal.

  • Out-migration scale — ~72 lakh unorganised workers on e-Shram (a floor; not migrant-tagged); 4.26 lakh returned during COVID; ~8 lakh Assamese estimated in Kerala. Assam Tribune; Sentinel Assam.
  • Job categories & skill split — Kerala construction: 56.5% helpers, 23.5% masons, ~20% skilled (survey). Perumbavoor plywood hub (1.5 lakh+ migrants) runs on Assamese labour. Core destination sectors: construction, plywood, seafood, hospitality, security, factories. IJSSHR; Deccan Herald.
  • Assam labour market — LFPR 66.9% (PLFS 2023–24); 21.16 lakh registered educated job-seekers (2024); 15.4 lakh registered unemployed (Jun 2026); dept. admits no in-state placement scheme. Assam Econ. Survey; Sentinel Assam.
  • The wage ladder — 81% of India's construction workforce unskilled (Knight Frank, 2023); Assam tea wage ₹280/day; statutory wage ladder ₹525→₹605 with certification.
  • The investment wave — Advantage Assam 2.0 (Feb 2025): ₹4.91 lakh Cr of commitments; Tata Electronics ₹27,000 Cr semiconductor OSAT at Jagiroad (~27,000 direct+indirect jobs projected, media-reported). Govt. of Assam; Tata Electronics.
  • Local skilling sectors & NSQF roles — retail, e-commerce, green energy, customer support, IT-ITeS, mining, media & marketing, tourism, apparel & handicrafts — all mapped to Sector Skill Council QP/NOS standards.
  • The skilling lesson — Karnataka CAG Report No. 6 of 2025 (CMKKY): 18% placement vs 70% target — the national case for demand-first skilling.
  • Schemes to build on — ASDM, Assam Skill University (Mangaldai), Orunodoi, CM's Atmanirbhar Asom self-employment scheme, CM Flight Scheme (global placement).
  • Commercials basis — indicative unit economics (₹5,000 blended per certified candidate; ₹12,000 per verified placement) adapted from BetterPlace's prior PMKVY 4.0 Special Project costing; to be finalised with the state.
  • BetterPlace company figures — present in 500+ cities; 30M+ workers, 2.5M+ skilled. Company-reported.

Migration "scale" figures are estimates or administrative floors — Assam keeps no census of its outbound workers. Commercials are indicative and to be finalised with the state. Company figures are company-reported. Outcome figures are targets.
